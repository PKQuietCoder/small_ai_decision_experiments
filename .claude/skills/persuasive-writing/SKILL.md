# Non-Technical Writing Rules

## Core Principle

Writing is applied psychology. The words are not the point. The effect the words have on the reader is the point. Focus on reception, not transmission.

## Rules

1. **Start with the reader's world, not yours.** Bad: "I built a tool that does X." Good: "You spend 3 hours every Friday updating spreadsheets. What if that took 30 seconds?" The reader should see themselves in the first line.

2. **First line is everything.** The title and opening sentence set the frame for everything after. If the first line doesn't hook, nothing else matters. Lead with a person, a scene, or a question -- never a feature.

3. **One core argument beats ten weak ones.** Find the single most compelling thing. Don't list features hoping one sticks. That is "engineering answer syndrome" -- listing points for part marks. In real life, there are no part marks.

4. **Stories beat statistics.** "92% satisfaction rate" -- nobody cares. "Riya used to spend all Friday updating reports. Last Friday she left at 3 PM." -- that lands.

5. **Validate before you redirect.** Start with what the reader already believes. Get them nodding. Then gently bend their view. Never open with a contradiction of their worldview.

6. **Use the audience's language.** LinkedIn sounds different from Reddit. WhatsApp sounds different from a pitch deck. Match the vocabulary of the world you're entering.

7. **Make it easy to enter.** Short sentences. Common words. No jargon without context. The brain rejects what it can't quickly parse. Simple writing is not dumb writing -- it is respectful writing.

8. **Ground everything with examples.** Abstract claims float away. Concrete examples stick. If you don't have a real example, build a prototype and use that.

9. **Show, then tell.** Open with a micro-story or scene. Then explain the point. Never open with the explanation.

10. **Omit needless words.** Every extra word dilutes the ones that matter. Cut "in order to", "it is important to note that", "basically", "actually", "really". Read your draft aloud. If you stumble, rewrite.

11. **Omit em dashes.** Em dashes make you sound like AI, replace them with proper punctuations.
